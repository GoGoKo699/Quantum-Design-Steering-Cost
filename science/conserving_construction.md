# A finite conserving construction

The reference bound is attained by a finite measurement that resolves label counts and then reads phases within a fixed charge sector. For every integer $K\geq1$, the construction produces

$$
|\psi_{N,\phi}\rangle
=\sum_{i=1}^D\sqrt{\frac{N_i}{K}}\,e^{i\phi_i}|i\rangle,
\qquad
N\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(K;1/D,\ldots,1/D),
$$

using a reference with QFI $4(K-1)V_A$. Independent phases chosen from three values suffice. At $K=D+1$, these states form an exact complex projective 2-design and their reference cost is $4D V_A$, matching the [converse](reference_bound.md).

The construction uses standard multinomial moments, amplitude and phase averaging, and charge-sector measurement methods. The connection of these ingredients to earlier designs and conserving POVM feasibility is recorded in [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md). For example, Appendix B.3 of [Navascués and Popescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.2101) describes a charge-block feasibility formulation for nondegenerate spectra. The explicit argument below establishes the stated implementation including degenerate charges.

## 1. Reference and charge accounting

Use the [paired basis and input state](model.md)

$$
Q_A|i\rangle=q_i|i\rangle,
\qquad
Q_B|i\rangle=-q_i|i\rangle,
\qquad
|\Omega\rangle_{AB}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt D}\sum_{i=1}^D|i\rangle_A|i\rangle_B.
$$

For the moment, retain $K-1$ reference factors, each of dimension $D$:

$$
|+_D\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt D}\sum_{i=1}^D|i\rangle,
\qquad
|r_K\rangle_R=|+_D\rangle^{\otimes(K-1)},
\qquad
Q_R=-\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}\sum_{i=1}^Dq_i|i\rangle\langle i|_{R_a}.
$$

Identities on the other reference factors are implicit in the last expression. When $K=1$, $R$ is one-dimensional, its state is fixed, and $Q_R=0$.

Each reference factor has the uniform label-charge distribution, mean charge $-\mu$, and variance $V_A$. Independence and purity therefore give

$$
F_Q(|r_K\rangle,Q_R)
=4\mathrm{Var}_{r_K}(Q_R)
=4(K-1)V_A.
$$

The negative reference charges are allowed by the model. If a nonnegative finite energy spectrum is desired, a scalar shift of each reference charge achieves it without changing the variance, QFI, or commutation relations. For arbitrary real $q_i$, the symmetry is the real-parameter group generated by total charge. A compact $U(1)$ interpretation additionally requires a common system-apparatus charge lattice after scalar shifts, as specified in the model. The reference constructed here shares the system lattice whenever one exists.

## 2. Count sectors and the occupied subspace

Label a basis vector of $BR$ by a string $(i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_{K-1})$, where $i_0$ labels $B$. Let

$$
\mathcal N_K
=\left\{N\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^D:\ \sum_iN_i=K\right\}.
$$

For $N\in\mathcal N_K$, let $P_N$ project onto the span of all such strings with label-count vector $N$. These projectors resolve the identity on $BR$. Their subspaces have a single total charge

$$
T=Q_B+Q_R,
\qquad
TP_N=t_NP_N,
\qquad
t_N=-\sum_iq_iN_i.
$$

Different count vectors can have the same charge, including when the $q_i$ are degenerate. Resolving them is still allowed: every $P_N$ commutes with $T$. A count measurement refines the charge sectors without connecting distinct charges.

Set

$$
C_N=\frac{K!}{\prod_jN_j!},
\qquad
I_N=\{i:N_i>0\}.
$$

For $i\in I_N$, the number of reference strings with count vector $N-e_i$ is

$$
C_{i,N}
=\frac{(K-1)!}{(N_i-1)!\prod_{j\ne i}N_j!}
=\frac{N_i}{K}C_N.
$$

Define the normalized vector

$$
|u_{i,N}\rangle
=|i\rangle_B\otimes
\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{i,N}}}
\sum_{\substack{(i_1,\ldots,i_{K-1})\\
\mathrm{count}(i_1,\ldots,i_{K-1})=N-e_i}}
|i_1,\ldots,i_{K-1}\rangle_R.
$$

For fixed $N$, these vectors are orthonormal because their $B$ labels differ. They also lie in orthogonal subspaces for distinct $N$. Let

$$
P_N^{\mathrm{occ}}
=\sum_{i\in I_N}|u_{i,N}\rangle\langle u_{i,N}|.
$$

The initial state has support only on these occupied subspaces. More explicitly,

$$
(I_A\otimes P_N)(|\Omega\rangle_{AB}\otimes|r_K\rangle_R)
=D^{-K/2}\sum_{i\in I_N}
\sqrt{C_{i,N}}\,|i\rangle_A|u_{i,N}\rangle_{BR}.
$$

Its squared norm is

$$
w_N=\frac{C_N}{D^K},
$$

because $\sum_iC_{i,N}=C_N$. Thus the count outcome has exactly the multinomial distribution claimed above.

## 3. A finite phase POVM in each count sector

Choose the phase set

$$
\Phi_D=\left\{0,\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{4\pi}{3}\right\}^{D},
\qquad L=|\Phi_D|=3^D.
$$

For $\phi\in\Phi_D$, define

$$
|v_{N,\phi}\rangle
=\sum_{i\in I_N}e^{-i\phi_i}|u_{i,N}\rangle,
\qquad
M_{N,\phi}=\frac{1}{L}|v_{N,\phi}\rangle\langle v_{N,\phi}|.
$$

Every vector in this definition has charge $t_N$, so each effect commutes with $T$. Independent phase averaging gives

$$
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\phi\in\Phi_D}e^{i(\phi_j-\phi_i)}=\delta_{ij},
\qquad
\sum_{\phi\in\Phi_D}M_{N,\phi}=P_N^{\mathrm{occ}}.
$$

Add the positive complementary effect

$$
M_{\perp}=I_{BR}-\sum_{N\in\mathcal N_K}P_N^{\mathrm{occ}}.
$$

The resulting family is a POVM on the full Hilbert space, and every effect is invariant. The complement has probability zero on the supplied input, since the initial state lies in the occupied subspaces. It is included in the measurement; no failure outcome is discarded. Equivalently, one can first measure $N$ and then perform the displayed phase POVM, completed on the unused part of that count subspace.

The phase values are labels for invariant measurement effects. They do not denote an additional external phase reference. The implementation in Section 6 uses a pointer on which the charge generator is zero.

## 4. Conditional states and probabilities

The rank-one effect has a particularly direct contraction with the input:

$$
\begin{aligned}
|a_{N,\phi}\rangle_A
&:=\frac{1}{\sqrt L}
(I_A\otimes\langle v_{N,\phi}|)
(|\Omega\rangle_{AB}\otimes|r_K\rangle_R)\\
&=\frac{D^{-K/2}}{\sqrt L}
\sum_{i\in I_N}\sqrt{C_{i,N}}\,e^{i\phi_i}|i\rangle_A\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{w_N}{L}}
\sum_{i=1}^D\sqrt{\frac{N_i}{K}}\,e^{i\phi_i}|i\rangle_A.
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently

$$
\tau_{N,\phi}=|a_{N,\phi}\rangle\langle a_{N,\phi}|,
\qquad
p_{N,\phi}=\frac{w_N}{L},
\qquad
\rho_{N,\phi}=|\psi_{N,\phi}\rangle\langle\psi_{N,\phi}|.
$$

The conditional states are normalized because $\sum_iN_i/K=1$. This proves the full outcome law, including the independence and uniformity of the phase labels. Some distinct labels can describe the same ray, for example because of a global phase or a zero occupation. Retaining or merging these duplicate labels leaves the ensemble moments unchanged.

## 5. Exact design and equality in the reference bound

Write $p_i=N_i/K$ within a sampled state. The ordinary multinomial identities are

$$
\mathbb E p_i=\frac1D,
\qquad
\mathbb E p_i^2=\frac{K+D-1}{KD^2},
\qquad
\mathbb E p_ip_j=\frac{K-1}{KD^2}\quad(i\ne j).
$$

The first identity and phase averaging give $\mathbb E\rho_{N,\phi}=I_A/D$. For the second moment, a matrix element contains a phase

$$
e^{i(\phi_i+\phi_j-\phi_k-\phi_l)}.
$$

The exponent of each individual phase is an integer between $-2$ and $2$. Averaging over three roots of unity therefore leaves this factor nonzero exactly when the two multisets $\{i,j\}$ and $\{k,l\}$ agree. Thus three phases per coordinate reproduce all phase averages needed for the second moment.

Let $\mathsf S$ swap the two copies of $A$, and let

$$
\Pi_{\mathrm{diag}}=\sum_{i=1}^D|ii\rangle\langle ii|.
$$

Combining the phase rule with the multinomial moments gives

$$
\boxed{
M_K:=\mathbb E\rho_{N,\phi}^{\otimes2}
=\frac{K-1}{KD^2}(I+\mathsf S)
+\frac{D+1-K}{KD^2}\Pi_{\mathrm{diag}}.
}
$$

Indeed, the coefficient on $|ii\rangle\langle ii|$ is $\mathbb E p_i^2$, and for each $i\ne j$ the coefficients on $|ij\rangle\langle ij|$ and $|ij\rangle\langle ji|$ are both $\mathbb E p_ip_j$. This checks every matrix element. When $K>D+1$, the second displayed coefficient is negative, but the full operator remains positive as its defining ensemble average shows.

At $K=D+1$, the diagonal correction vanishes:

$$
M_{D+1}=\frac{I+\mathsf S}{D(D+1)}.
$$

The output is therefore a finite weighted exact complex projective 2-design. Its resource cost is

$$
F_Q(|r_{D+1}\rangle,Q_R)=4D V_A.
$$

This proves attainment of the exact minimum in [Theorem 3 and its consequence](reference_bound.md#5-the-attained-optimum-and-its-scope). The design is selected by the construction; no existence assumption about SICs or complete sets of mutually unbiased bases is needed.

The construction also saturates the pure-ensemble information bound at every integer $K$. The conditional charge mean is

$$
m_N=\langle\psi_{N,\phi}|Q_A|\psi_{N,\phi}\rangle
=\frac1K\sum_iq_iN_i.
$$

It is the sample mean of $K$ independent uniformly chosen charges. Hence

$$
\mathrm{Var}(m_N)=\frac{V_A}{K}
=\frac{V_A^2}{V_A+F_Q(|r_K\rangle,Q_R)/4}
\quad\text{when }V_A>0.
$$

Its average output QFI is $4V_A(K-1)/K$, equal to the mixed-output upper bound for this reference. The [accuracy analysis](accuracy.md) derives the second-moment error and its operational qualifications from $M_K$.

## 6. A conserving unitary and neutral pointer

Individually invariant effects have a realization with no additional initial asymmetry. This can be proved for any finite POVM $\{M_x\}_{x\in\mathcal X}$ satisfying $[M_x,T]=0$, including the one above.

Take a pointer $P$ with orthonormal outcome states $\{|x\rangle:x\in\mathcal X\}$ and charge $Q_P=0$. Choose a fixed initial pointer state $|\mathrm{ready}\rangle$. Define an isometry from $BR$ to $BRP$ by

$$
W|\eta\rangle
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}M_x^{1/2}|\eta\rangle\otimes|x\rangle.
$$

Completeness gives $W^\dagger W=I_{BR}$. Functional calculus gives $[M_x^{1/2},T]=0$, so

$$
(T\otimes I_P)W=WT.
$$

For each charge eigenspace $\mathcal H_t\subseteq\mathcal H_{BR}$, the map $|\eta\rangle|\mathrm{ready}\rangle\mapsto W|\eta\rangle$ is an isometry between two subspaces of the same finite-dimensional space $\mathcal H_t\otimes\mathcal H_P$. Extend orthonormal bases of these two subspaces to orthonormal bases of that full space. Mapping one completed basis to the other gives a unitary $U_t$. Combining the blocks gives a unitary $U$ such that

$$
U(|\eta\rangle\otimes|\mathrm{ready}\rangle)=W|\eta\rangle,
\qquad
[U,T\otimes I_P]=0.
$$

Measurement of $P$ in the outcome basis produces the effects $M_x$ on $BR$. Both its preparation and readout are neutral because $Q_P=0$. The full operation on $ABRP$ is $I_A\otimes U$, and it conserves $Q_A+Q_B+Q_R+Q_P$ exactly. It acts trivially on $A$ throughout. Discarding $BRP$ after recording the outcome gives precisely the conditional ensemble computed above.

This is a finite, charge-block version of the usual measurement dilation. It establishes existence of an allowed apparatus, with arbitrary collective conserving interactions as specified by the model. It does not impose a circuit-size, locality, or interaction-time guarantee.

## 7. Compression to one reference vector per charge

The tensor-product reference is convenient for the count proof, but its full Hilbert-space dimension is unnecessary. Let

$$
\mathcal S_K
=\left\{-\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}q_{i_a}:i_a\in\{1,\ldots,D\}\right\},
$$

with $\mathcal S_1=\{0\}$. For $s\in\mathcal S_K$, define

$$
r_s
=D^{-(K-1)}
\#\left\{(i_1,\ldots,i_{K-1}):-\sum_aq_{i_a}=s\right\}.
$$

All $r_s$ in this set are positive. Let $|\chi_s\rangle$ be the normalized equal superposition of the reference strings counted in $r_s$. Then

$$
Q_R|\chi_s\rangle=s|\chi_s\rangle,
\qquad
|r_K\rangle=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_K}\sqrt{r_s}|\chi_s\rangle.
$$

Choose a compressed reference $\widetilde R$ with one orthonormal vector $|s\rangle$ for each distinct value $s$, charge

$$
Q_{\widetilde R}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_K}s|s\rangle\langle s|,
\qquad
|\widetilde r_K\rangle=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_K}\sqrt{r_s}|s\rangle.
$$

The isometry $J|s\rangle=|\chi_s\rangle$ satisfies $Q_RJ=JQ_{\widetilde R}$ and $J|\widetilde r_K\rangle=|r_K\rangle$. Pull back every effect:

$$
\widetilde M_x=(I_B\otimes J^\dagger)M_x(I_B\otimes J).
$$

These are positive and sum to $I_{B\widetilde R}$. The charge-intertwining identity implies

$$
[\widetilde M_x,Q_B+Q_{\widetilde R}]
=(I_B\otimes J^\dagger)[M_x,Q_B+Q_R](I_B\otimes J)=0.
$$

Embedding the compressed reference with $J$ also shows that every unnormalized conditional state of $A$ is unchanged. To see this without making any claim about the square root of a pulled-back effect, use the dependence of a remote conditional state on the effect alone:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde\tau_x
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{B\widetilde R}\!\left[
(I_A\otimes\widetilde M_x)
(|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|\otimes
|\widetilde r_K\rangle\langle\widetilde r_K|)
\right]\\
&=\mathrm{Tr}_{BR}\!\left[
(I_A\otimes M_x)
(|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|\otimes|r_K\rangle\langle r_K|)
\right]
=\tau_x.
\end{aligned}
$$

The compressed state has the same charge distribution, so it has the same variance and QFI. This proves compression as an alternative protocol; it does not require preparing the larger reference and physically compressing it during the task. Section 6 supplies a conserving neutral-pointer realization for the pulled-back POVM as well.

The number of distinct compressed levels obeys

$$
|\mathcal S_K|\leq {K+D-2\choose D-1},
$$

because each charge is specified by a count vector of $K-1$ labels. Charge coincidences can lower this dimension. For $m$ qubits with excitation charge and $D=2^m$, $r_s$ is a binomial distribution with $m(K-1)$ trials, up to charge sign and shift. The compressed reference then has $m(K-1)+1$ charge levels. At the exact endpoint this is $mD+1$, and its QFI is $mD$.

## 8. Degeneracies and limiting cases

Charge degeneracy never identifies distinct system basis vectors: the labels $i$ remain orthogonal even when $q_i=q_j$. Count subspaces, the vectors $|u_{i,N}\rangle$, and phase completeness therefore remain well defined. If different reference strings or count vectors share a total charge, compression combines their probabilities in $r_s$ and the isometry proof preserves the full measurement. No nondegeneracy assumption has entered the construction.

At $K=1$, the output is the charge eigenbasis ensemble with uniform probabilities. Its QFI cost is zero, and $M_1=\Pi_{\mathrm{diag}}/D$. When $V_A=0$, all system charges coincide. The compressed reference has one level for every $K$, and in particular the exact design at $K=D+1$ costs zero. The case $D=1$ is included in these statements.

All preparations, effects, and pointer spaces used here are finite. The reference is consumable, the complete record is neutral, and no conditional action on $A$ is performed. The attainable QFI minimum concerns this exact ensemble target; the construction does not supply a finite implementation for every prescribed design or optimize its other physical costs.
